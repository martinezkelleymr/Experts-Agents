## 1. Executive summary

**Market snapshot (October 2026 data, released November 16, 2026)** **[F]** [S1][S2][S3]

| Metric (single-family, Oct 2026) | Miami-Dade | Broward | Palm Beach |
|---|---|---|---|

| Closed sales (YoY) | 782 (−11.8%) | 892 (−13.9%) | 1,080 (from 1,253) |
| Active listings | 4,739 (−13.81%) | 4,467 (−16.72%) | 4,801 |
| Months of supply | 5.4 | 4.4 | 3.9 |

| Cash share of SF sales | 25.8% | 25.4% | 45.9% |
| Distressed share of all residential sales | 0.4% | 1.2% | 1.3% |

**Key findings**

1. **Prices are high and inventory is tightening, while sales volumes fall because of mortgage rates.** The Freddie Mac 30-year fixed average was **7.28%** on November 19, 2026, versus 6.26% a year earlier **[F]** [S6]. Single-family inventory is down 14–17% YoY in all three counties **[F]** [S1][S2][S3].
2. **Distress is near historic lows** (0.4%–1.3% of sales) **[F]** [S1][S2][S3]. Discounted inventory must be *sourced* (off-market, probate, code-violation, tired landlords, functionally obsolete homes), not bought from foreclosure lists.
3. **Buy-and-hold rentals at median prices do not cash-flow with today's debt.** An illustrative Broward calculation using official HUD Fair Market Rent, actual county millage, and Citizens' average landlord premium produces an unlevered yield of roughly **2.8%** and a negative levered cash flow (Section 5.5) **[E]**. Rental returns must come from buying below market, adding value (renovation, ADUs, additional units), or development at cost.
4. **Florida's 2026 property-tax amendment changes the math for investors.** Amendment 3 (approved November 3, 2026, ~62%) raises the non-school homestead exemption to $150,000 (2027) and $250,000 (2028) and **cuts the annual assessment-increase cap for non-homestead property (including rentals) from 10% to 5%** **[F]** [S7][S8]. Implementation dates for the 5% cap are **[TBV]**.
5. **Insurance costs are easing.** Citizens' 2026 rates fell an average of 8.8% for homeowners multiperil; OIR reported average requested homeowners rate changes of −4.8% (30-day average) in September 2026 **[F]** [S10][S11].
6. **Land is scarce and expensive in the urban core.** Miami-Dade is bounded by the Urban Development Boundary (UDB) and the Everglades; the Palm Beach Agricultural Reserve is tightly controlled **[F]** [S20][S22]. Infill lots, teardown sites, and Live Local Act sites are the realistic entry points for a small developer.
7. **New-home production is dominated by a few national/regional builders.** In 2025, Lennar held ~36.7% of the Miami–Fort Lauderdale–West Palm Beach new-home closings market, followed by GL Homes (12.6%) and D.R. Horton (10.8%) **[E]** (Builder magazine) [S26]. A start-up builder should not compete head-on on master-planned subdivisions.
8. **Federal law now restricts large institutional SFR buyers** (≥350 homes) via the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (Public Law 119-101, July 11, 2026) **[F]** [S30][S31]. This does not restrict MK.Buildings at launch, and it creates exceptions (renovate-to-rent, build-to-rent, new construction for sale) that align with MK.Buildings' model — but it may reduce institutional exit buyers for stabilized single-family rentals.
9. **AI advantage is real but must be workflow-based.** McKinsey estimates AI could create $430B–$550B in value across the real estate value chain and stresses redesigning entire workflows rather than adding isolated tools **[E]** [S38]. MK.Buildings can be built AI-native from day one.

**Recommended direction (business assumption [A]):** Launch with a disciplined *value-add acquisition* engine (light-to-heavy renovations for resale or rental), use that cash flow and local knowledge to move into *small infill development* (single-family spec homes, duplexes/ADUs, small multifamily), and build the AI-supported analysis platform alongside the first deals. Details in Section 14.

## 4. Property values and comparable sales

### 4.1 Medians are not comps

County medians (Section 3) describe the market; they do not value a property. MK.Buildings must value each property from **closed comparable sales** (same submarket, similar size/age/lot/condition, recent dates) and adjust for differences.

### 4.2 Official and professional data sources for comps

| Source | What it provides | Access | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Miami-Dade Property Appraiser (miamidadepa.gov) | Ownership, assessed/just value, sale history, millage, folio data | Public web; bulk data products | [F] [S12] |
| Broward County Property Appraiser (bcpa.net) | Same for Broward; millage tables | Public web | [F] [S13] |
| Palm Beach County Property Appraiser (pbcpao.gov) | Same for Palm Beach; TRIM notices, proposed millage | Public web | [F] [S14] |
| County Clerks of Court (official records) | Deeds, mortgages, liens, lis pendens, probate | Public web | [TBV per county portal] |
| MIAMI MLS / BeachesMLS (merged with MIAMI REALTORS® + RWorld, May 11, 2026) | Active/pending/closed listings, DOM, photos, rents | Requires broker/agent membership and data license | [F] [S1] |
| SFMarketIntel.com | County statistical reports | Published by the association | [F] [S1] |
| City of Miami open data (building permits) | Permits by address/folio, contractor activity | Public ArcGIS open data | [F] [S36] |
| Miami-Dade Open Data Hub | Future Land Use (CDMP) layers | Public | [F] [S21] |

### 4.3 Comparable-sales method (standard practice) **[A — internal procedure]**

1. Pull closed sales within the same subdivision/neighborhood, ideally ≤ 6 months old and ≤ 0.5–1 mile away (expand only when needed).
2. Match property type, bedrooms/bathrooms, living area (±15–20%), lot size, year built/effective age, pool, waterfront, garage.
3. Adjust for condition (renovated vs. original), flood zone, and roof/windows age (these affect insurance in Florida).
4. Reconcile to an **As-Is Value** and an **After-Repair Value (ARV)**.
5. Cross-check with list-to-sale ratios (94%–96% county medians in Oct 2026 **[F]**) and DOM.
6. Record every comp and adjustment in the deal file for audit (AI-agent friendly; see Section 15).

### 5.4 Florida landlord-tenant environment

- **State preemption:** CS/HB 1417 (2023) preempted regulation of residential tenancies to the state, superseding many local tenant ordinances **[F]** [S33].

- **Flood disclosure to tenants:** CS/CS/SB 948 (2025) created s. 83.512, F.S., requiring landlords to disclose known flood history and claims to prospective tenants; failure can allow a tenant to terminate after substantial flood loss **[F — per Senate bill analysis]** [S35]. Effective date **[TBV]**.

### 5.5 Illustrative rental return check (derived estimate) **[E]**

This is **not** an investment projection. It tests whether a median-priced Broward single-family home, rented at the HUD 3-BR FMR, works as a leveraged rental at today's costs. Each input is labeled.

| Line | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | $610,000 | Broward SF median, Oct 2026 **[F]** [S2] |
| Monthly rent | $3,216 | Broward 3-BR FMR FY2026 **[F]** [S15] |
| Gross annual rent | $38,592 | Derived |
| Vacancy/credit loss 5% | −$1,930 | **[A]** (multifamily occupancy ~95% **[F]** [S18] used as proxy) |
| Effective gross income | $36,662 | Derived |
| Property tax (19.0 mills × $610,000) | −$11,590 | Broward unincorporated code 0012 = 19.0149 mills **[F]** [S13]; assumes assessed ≈ price and no exemptions **[A]** |
| Insurance | −$2,138 | Citizens 2026 avg approved DP1/DP3 premium, Broward **[F]** [S9]; actual quote will vary; flood insurance excluded **[A]** |
| Property management 8% of EGI | −$2,933 | **[A]** |
| Maintenance & capital reserve 8% of gross rent | −$3,087 | **[A]** |
| **Net operating income (NOI)** | **≈ $16,914** | Derived |
| **Unlevered yield (cap rate)** | **≈ 2.8%** | Derived |
| Loan 75% LTV ($457,500), 30-yr at 7.00% | −$36,526/yr | Rate within advertised DSCR range 6.375%–7.125% **[E]** [S28] |
| **Annual cash flow after debt** | **≈ −$19,600** | Derived |
| Debt service coverage ratio | ≈ 0.46 | Derived (most DSCR lenders ask for 0.75–1.25 **[E]** [S28]) |

**Conclusion [E]:** At today's prices, taxes, and interest rates, a median Broward home at FMR-level rent is strongly negative-leverage. Same direction for Miami-Dade (3-BR FMR × 12 ÷ median price ≈ 5.3% gross yield) and Palm Beach (≈ 5.1%) **[E]**. Rental profitability therefore depends on:
- Buying materially below market value (distressed condition, off-market, motivated sellers).
- Forcing value via renovation, adding bedrooms/baths, legal ADUs, or converting to compliant 2–4 unit properties.
- Developing at cost (build-to-rent) so that value exceeds cost.
- Targeting lower-priced submarkets with higher rent-to-price ratios (to be identified by the Market Research Analyst).
- Lower leverage, or refinancing when rates fall (rate-dependent; not assumed).

## 6. Fix-and-flip market

### 6.1 National benchmarks (ATTOM Q2 2026 Home Flipping Report, published October 1, 2026) **[F]** [S24]

| Metric | Q2 2026 |
|---|---|
| Homes flipped (U.S.) | 77,991 |
| Flips as share of all sales | 6.2% |
| Typical gross profit (resale minus purchase price) | $60,526 |
| Typical gross margin / ROI on purchase price | 21.5% |
| Average days to flip | 161 |

**Critical caveat:** ATTOM's "gross profit" is resale price minus purchase price. It **excludes** renovation, holding, financing, insurance, taxes, and selling costs. Net returns are much lower **[F — definition per ATTOM]**.

A secondary source reports Florida Q2 2026 at 8,192 flips, ~$68,000 typical gross profit, and a 24.1% gross return **[E — secondary summary of ATTOM; TBV against ATTOM state table]** [S25]. Tri-county metro-level flip data was not available in public excerpts **[TBV — obtain ATTOM data license]**.

### 6.2 Flip economics framework **[A — internal underwriting standard]**

```
Maximum Allowable Offer (MAO) =
    ARV
  − Renovation budget (incl. contingency 10–15%)
  − Acquisition closing costs (title, doc stamps on deed if buyer pays per contract, lender fees)
  − Holding costs (interest, taxes, insurance, utilities, HOA) × planned months
  − Selling costs (brokerage commissions, seller closing costs, doc stamps on deed (customarily seller-paid in much of Florida — TBV per contract), concessions)
  − Target profit (company minimum)
```

Florida-specific cost items to include: documentary stamp tax on deeds ($0.70 per $100; Miami-Dade $0.60 per $100 for single-family residences) and on mortgages ($0.35 per $100), plus nonrecurring intangible tax on mortgages (0.2%) **[F]** [S27a][S27b]. Florida Building Code wind requirements (impact windows/doors, roof) materially affect renovation budgets and insurance pricing (Section 9.3).

**Interpretation [E]:** Large tracts are being absorbed by large builders at prices a start-up cannot easily compete with. MK.Buildings' realistic land opportunities are:
1. **Infill single-family lots and teardowns** inside established neighborhoods (zoned residential, utilities present).
2. **Lots allowing duplexes/townhomes or small multifamily** under existing zoning.
3. **Live Local Act sites** (commercial/industrial/mixed-use zoned land where residential can be approved administratively if ≥40% of units are affordable; Sec. 9.2) — likely a later-phase strategy because of capital needs.
4. **Land with entitlement upside** (rezoning, platting, variances) — higher risk, longer timelines.

Land pricing must be established parcel-by-parcel from property-appraiser deed records (vacant land use codes), MLS land sales, and broker opinions. **No reliable public per-acre or per-lot benchmark for small infill lots by county was found in this research [TBV].**

**Production-builder benchmark (revenue side) [E]:** Lennar's 2025 South Florida closings averaged **$537,180** for **2,303 sq ft** (≈ **$233/sq ft of sale price**, derived); D.R. Horton averaged $533,499 for 1,782 sq ft (≈ $299/sq ft) [S26b]. These are *sale prices* that include land, so production builders' hard costs are well below small-builder quotes. **Implication:** a small builder cannot compete on cost with production subdivisions; it must target infill locations where finished-home values per sq ft are high and production builders are absent.

**Required next step [TBV]:** Obtain 3+ written GC bids on a standard plan (e.g., 2,000–2,500 sq ft CBS single-family) in each county, plus RSMeans or equivalent licensed cost data, before any land purchase.

### 8.2 Soft costs and timeline items to budget **[A]**

Architecture & engineering; surveys and geotechnical; permit and plan-review fees; impact fees; utility connection fees; builder's risk and general liability insurance; construction loan interest and fees; property taxes during construction; marketing and sales commissions; contingency (hard and soft).

### 8.3 Development feasibility framework **[A — internal standard]**

**Residual land value method** (how much MK.Buildings can pay for land):

```
Residual Land Value =
    Gross Development Value (GDV: projected sale prices, or stabilized value for rentals)
  − Hard costs
  − Soft costs
  − Financing costs
  − Sales/closing costs
  − Developer profit target (e.g., % of cost or % of GDV)
```

**Build-to-rent test:** Stabilized NOI ÷ total project cost = **yield-on-cost**. Proceed only if yield-on-cost exceeds the market cap rate for comparable stabilized product by a margin set by the founder (the "development spread"). Market cap-rate references in Section 5.2 are for multifamily and must be replaced with single-family/small-multifamily evidence **[TBV]**.

## 9. Zoning, permitting, building regulations, and environmental considerations

### 9.1 Permit timelines — state "shot clock" (s. 553.792, F.S., 2025 Statutes) **[F]** [S31b]

| Permit type (local plans reviewer) | Deadline after complete application |
|---|---|
| Residential units incl. single-family, < 7,500 sq ft | 30 business days |
| Residential units ≥ 7,500 sq ft | 60 business days |
| Multifamily ≤ 50 units; site plans/plats without hearing; lot grading | 60 business days |
| Site-specific permit under a master building permit | 12 business days |

- Local government must notify within 5 business days what is missing **[F]**.
- Missed deadlines trigger fee reductions of 10% per business day (20% per day after revisions deadlines) unless extended or applicant-caused **[F]**.
- Florida also allows private-provider plan review and inspections (s. 553.791, F.S.) **[TBV — confirm current provisions]**.

### 9.2 Live Local Act (Miami-Dade County memo, March 25, 2026) **[F]** [S22]

- Requires ≥ **40% of units** as affordable rentals (as defined in s. 420.0004, F.S.) for at least **30 years**; the association summary describes "affordable" as up to 120% of AMI **[F]** [S1].
- Applies to land zoned commercial, industrial, or mixed use (e.g., Miami-Dade BU, IU, RU-5, RU-5A), plus certain religious-institution and (from July 1, 2026, HB 1389) local-government and school-district land.
- Local governments cannot restrict density below the highest allowed in the jurisdiction (Miami-Dade max cited at 250 units/acre in Metropolitan Urban Centers) or height below the tallest allowed within one mile (or three stories), with limits next to single-family neighborhoods.
- Mandatory parking reductions near transit; approval through **administrative** site plan review (no rezoning, variance, or hearing required).
- SB 1730 (2025) added administrative demolition approval, anti-circumvention rules, and attorney-fee recovery up to $250,000 for developers who prevail **[F]**.

### 9.3 Florida Building Code and wind design **[F]**

- **9th Edition Florida Building Code (2026) becomes effective December 31, 2026** (Miami-Dade notice on product approvals) [S32]. Projects permitted after that date will be designed to the 9th Edition — confirm transition rules with each building department **[TBV]**.
- Miami-Dade and Broward are in the **High-Velocity Hurricane Zone (HVHZ)** as defined in the Florida Building Code (confirm the exact code section **[TBV]**); Miami-Dade requires product approvals (NOAs) for building envelope products [S32]. HVHZ requirements increase costs for windows, doors, and roofing **[E]**.

### 9.4 Flood and climate **[F]**

- **Seller flood disclosure:** s. 689.302, F.S., requires sellers of residential property to disclose known flooding damage, flood insurance claims, and FEMA/other flood assistance at or before contract [S35a].
- **Landlord flood disclosure:** see Section 5.4 [S35].
- **Sea level rise:** the Southeast Florida Regional Climate Change Compact's unified projection (2019) cited by local governments projects **10–17 inches by 2040 and 21–54 inches by 2070** above 2000 mean sea level (Key West gauge); the Compact began reviewing the 2019 projection against NOAA's 2022 report in late 2024 **[F]** [S37].
- FEMA flood zone, base flood elevation, and local freeboard requirements must be checked per parcel **[A]**; flood insurance is separate from homeowners/dwelling policies.

### 9.5 Federal institutional-investor law **[F]** [S30][S31]

- Executive Order 14376 (January 20, 2026) declared that large institutional investors should not buy single-family homes families could buy.
- The **21st Century ROAD to Housing Act** became law **July 11, 2026** (Public Law 119-101). It bars "large institutional investors" — for-profit entities with investment control of **≥ 350 single-family homes** — from buying single-family homes, with exceptions including: homes newly built/renovated **for sale**; **build-to-rent** programs; **renovate-to-rent** programs that rehab homes not meeting code with improvements ≥ 15% of purchase price; certain rent-to-own and homeownership programs; and other enumerated cases (Morgan Lewis summary [S31a]).
- **Relevance:** MK.Buildings is far below the threshold. However, the act may reduce institutional demand when MK.Buildings sells stabilized rental homes, while preserving demand for build-to-rent and renovate-to-rent product. Legal review needed before any portfolio sale **[A]**.

### 9.6 Condominium and HOA rules

**Condominiums (SB 4-D 2022, SB 154 2023, HB 913 2025)** **[F]** [S40][S41]
- Milestone structural inspections for condo/co-op buildings **three or more habitable stories**, at 30 years (local option for 25 years near the coast), then every 10 years.
- Structural Integrity Reserve Studies (SIRS) with mandatory, non-waivable reserves for structural elements; HB 913 added a baseline funding plan, raised the reserve-item threshold from $10,000 to $25,000, and allowed temporary reserve pauses to fund milestone repairs.
- **Implication:** condo units carry special-assessment risk; reflected in weak condo pricing (Sec. 3). Avoid condos in Phase 1 **[A]**.

**HOAs (HB 1203, effective July 1, 2024)** **[F]** [S42]
- HOAs with ≥ 100 parcels must post official records online (by Jan 1, 2025).
- Fines capped at $100 per violation and $1,000 aggregate (unless documents allow more); hearing within 90 days; no fine if cured before hearing.
- HOAs cannot regulate interiors not visible from frontage/adjacent property.
- About 45% of Florida homes are in an HOA (House staff analysis) **[F]**.
- **HOA fee levels:** no reliable county-wide averages were found; collect fees and estoppel/special assessment status per community **[TBV]**.

| Construction control | Budget vs. actual tracking, schedule risk alerts, photo-based progress checks | Reduce overruns and holding time |
| Property operations | Maintenance triage, rent collection follow-up, renewal pricing | Lower opex per unit; better tenant experience |
| Proprietary cost database | Every bid and actual cost captured | Over time, more accurate budgets than public data |
| Learning loop | Post-mortems compare underwritten vs. actual results; models recalibrate | Compounding institutional knowledge |

### 16.3 Guardrails and risks **[A]**
- **Accuracy:** AI estimates must cite data and show confidence ranges; humans verify comps and budgets.
- **Fair Housing:** no use of protected characteristics in tenant screening, pricing, or marketing; review automated screening for disparate impact.
- **Data licensing:** MLS data use is governed by MLS rules and broker agreements; respect website terms and privacy law.
- **Securities and licensing:** AI may not give investment solicitations or perform licensed brokerage activities without proper licensing and legal review.
- **Security:** protect tenant and investor personal data.
